# Planet in a nutshell

## The problem

Travel information is everywhere, but almost none of it is about *your* trip. Guidebooks and websites tell you what's worth seeing in a city. Weather apps tell you whether it will rain. Currency apps tell you what a euro is worth. Maps tell you how to get somewhere. You end up doing the joining-up yourself: flicking between apps, converting prices in your head, and working out whether that museum is worth the trip from your hotel on the one rainy afternoon you have.

## What Planet does

Planet blends three kinds of information into one plan that fits you:

1. **Your trip.** Where you're going, your dates, where you're staying, your home currency, and what you enjoy: your interests, budget, pace and who you're travelling with.
2. **Static information: things that rarely change.** The places worth visiting, roughly what they cost, when they're open, and practical local tips.
3. **Dynamic information: things that change all the time.** The weather forecast, today's exchange rates and, while you're travelling, where you actually are right now.

The first gives Planet its personal angle, the second gives it substance, and the third keeps it current.

## Why the blend matters

Each piece on its own is easy to find. The value is in combining them:

- A list of top sights becomes **a day-by-day plan** built around where you're staying and how much you like to walk.
- A price in drachmas, lira or yen becomes **a price you understand**, in your own currency at today's rate.
- A rainy forecast becomes **a suggestion** to swap the outdoor ruins for the museum that afternoon.
- Your location becomes **"what's near me now"** and directions from where you're standing, not from a generic starting point.

## In one sentence

Planet is a travel guide that knows your trip: it takes what's worth doing at your destination and shapes it around your dates, your base, your money and your tastes, then keeps adjusting as the weather, exchange rates and your location change.
